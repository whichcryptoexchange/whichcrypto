There's no single best place to get a crypto licence. It depends on where your customers are, how much substance you can put on the ground, and who you need to convince: banks, payment partners or institutional clients. This page compares the regimes, with links to a full guide for each.

## The headline comparison

| | EU (MiCA) | UK (FCA) | Dubai (VARA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Crypto-specific authorisation[^art59] | AML registration today, FSMA authorisation from 2027[^fca-regime] | Crypto-specific licence[^vara] |
| Reach | All EU/EEA states by passport[^art65] | UK only | Dubai mainland and free zones, not DIFC[^vara] |
| Minimum capital | €50,000 to €150,000, or ¼ of fixed overheads if higher[^art67][^annex4] | No prescribed minimum for registration[^fca-app] | AED 100,000 to AED 1.5m+, by activity[^vara-capital] |
| Fees | Set nationally, e.g. €3,000 in Estonia, time-based in Germany[^fi][^bafin] | £11,260, non-refundable[^fca-fee][^fca-fees] | AED 40,000 to 200,000 per activity, plus annual supervision fees[^vara-fees] |
| Decision timeline | 25 working days to check completeness, then 40 to decide[^art63] | 3 months once complete[^fca-how] | Two-stage process[^vara-apps] |
| Substance | EU registered office and management, at least one EU-resident director[^art59] | Business carried on in the UK[^fca-fincrime] | Dubai office, two full-time UAE-resident responsible individuals[^vara-faq] |
| Who holds it (our register) | See the EU table below | {{country_count:GB}} brands | {{country_count:AE}} brands (Dubai and Abu Dhabi) |

## Choosing an EU home state

MiCA's rules are the same in every member state, but the practicalities differ:

| Home state | Authority | Transition chosen[^grandfathering] | Entries on our register | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | BaFin | 12 months | {{home_count:DE}} | [Germany](/learn/crypto-licence-germany-bafin/) |
| Cyprus | CySEC | 18 months | {{home_count:CY}} | [Cyprus](/learn/crypto-licence-cyprus/) |
| Malta | MFSA | 18 months | {{home_count:MT}} | [Malta](/learn/crypto-licence-malta/) |
| Lithuania | Bank of Lithuania | 12 months | {{home_count:LT}} | [Lithuania](/learn/crypto-licence-lithuania/) |
| Slovakia | NBS | 12 months | {{home_count:SK}} | [Slovakia](/learn/crypto-licence-slovakia/) |
| Estonia | Finantsinspektsioon | 18 months | {{home_count:EE}} | [Estonia](/learn/crypto-licence-estonia/) |

Register entries combine full CASP authorisations with notifications by existing banks and investment firms, because ESMA's register lists both. Every transitional period has now ended.[^art143]

## Questions to answer before you choose

1. **Where are your customers?** If they're across the EU, MiCA's passport is the only route here that reaches them all. ESMA says firms outside the EU "cannot provide MiCA services to EU clients or solicit EU clients", including business clients.[^wind]
2. **How much substance can you put on the ground?** MiCA requires real EU management and an EU-resident director.[^art59] Dubai requires a local office and two UAE-resident responsible individuals.[^vara-faq]
3. **Who will check your licence?** Banks and partners can read the same evidence you can: transition choices, peer reviews, approval rates. See [what the evidence says](/learn/crypto-licence-reputation-evidence/).
4. **Can you work in the regulator's language?** Estonia requires applications in Estonian.[^fi] Much German, Slovak and Cypriot guidance is published in the national language.

## Shortcuts that no longer work

Buying a company that holds an old national crypto registration doesn't give you MiCA authorisation. Estonia's old FIU licences, for example, all lost validity on 1 July 2026.[^fiu-vasp] Since the MiCA transition ended, ESMA has told unauthorised firms to stop onboarding EU clients and wind down.[^wind]
