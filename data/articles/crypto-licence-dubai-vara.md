Dubai has a regulator dedicated to virtual assets. A VARA licence is a genuine crypto-specific licence, but it only covers Dubai, and it comes with real substance requirements. This guide uses VARA's own website and rulebooks.

## Scope

VARA is the "sole authority regulating virtual assets across Dubai's free zones and mainland, except within the jurisdiction of Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC)."[^vara] A VARA licence does not cover Abu Dhabi's ADGM, which has its own regulator, or the rest of the UAE.

VARA licenses eight activities: advisory, broker-dealer, custody, exchange, lending and borrowing, management and investment, transfer and settlement, and category 1 token issuance.[^activities]

## The two-stage process

"To apply for a VARA licence all applicants follow a two-step process."[^vara]

1. **Stage 1.** You submit an Initial Disclosure Questionnaire through Dubai Economy & Tourism (DET) for mainland firms, or through a Dubai free zone, and receive an Approval to Incorporate to set up your legal entity. "At this point, the firm is not permitted to carry on Virtual Asset activities."[^applications]
2. **Stage 2.** You complete the full application and "receive a VASP Licence, which may be subject to operational conditions."[^applications]

VARA's public register also shows firms holding an In-Principle Approval, which it describes as "a conditional step in the licensing process".[^register] VARA says an LLC or free-zone company "is preferable for this sector. Applications from sole proprietorships will not be accepted by VARA."[^faq]

## Costs and capital

VARA's fee schedule sets fees per licensed activity, from AED 40,000 (for example advisory, transfer and settlement) to AED 200,000, and "VASPs must pay an Annual Supervision Fee for each VA Activity Licensed".[^fees] VARA may add supervision fees "based upon a VASP's risk profile".[^fees]

Paid-up capital depends on the activity.[^capital] Some examples:

| Activity | Minimum paid-up capital |
|---|---|
| Advisory | AED 100,000 |
| Custody | higher of AED 600,000 or 25% of fixed annual overheads |
| Exchange (standalone) | higher of AED 1,500,000 or 25% of fixed annual overheads |

## Substance

This is where Dubai differs most from a passport-based model. "VARA requires VASPs to have a physical presence in Dubai to conduct VA activities from Dubai," including a private office for most activities.[^faq] You must appoint two Responsible Individuals, and "each Responsible Individual shall be a full-time employee of the VASP; a Fit and Proper Person; a resident of the UAE or a holder of a UAE passport."[^faq]

## Who has done it

VARA's public register lists 61 firms that are either fully licensed or hold In-Principle Approval.[^register] Our register tracks **{{country_count:AE}}** brands with a Dubai VARA or Abu Dhabi ADGM entry: see the [UAE page](/ae/).

## Enforcement

VARA publishes its enforcement actions. Most listed entries concern unlicensed activity, meaning firms carrying on or advertising virtual asset activities without a VARA licence. Others concern specific breaches, for example anti-money-laundering programme controls or marketing rules.[^enforcement] VARA's powers include written reprimands and "imposing fines or other civil penalties".[^enforcement]

## Pros and cons

**In Dubai's favour:**
- A dedicated, crypto-specific licence with a published rulebook and fee schedule.[^fees][^capital]
- A clear staged process, with incorporation approved before the full licence.[^applications]

**Things to weigh:**
- It covers Dubai only. There is no passport into the EU or elsewhere.[^vara]
- Real substance costs: a Dubai office, two full-time UAE-resident responsible individuals, and per-activity annual fees.[^faq][^fees]
- An active enforcement function that names firms publicly.[^enforcement]
