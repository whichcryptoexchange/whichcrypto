Lithuania appears on ESMA's MiCA register with a small but recognisable group of firms. This short guide covers what is confirmed from official sources.

*Note: the Bank of Lithuania's website could not be reviewed for this article, so national forms, fees and guidance aren't covered here. MiCA's common requirements apply in full.*

## The rules

Firms authorised in Lithuania appear on ESMA's MiCA register with the Bank of Lithuania as their competent authority.[^casps] The requirements are MiCA's own: an EU registered office, effective management in the EU, an EU-resident director, the Article 62 file and €50,000 to €150,000 minimum capital, decided within MiCA's 25-plus-40 working day timetable. See [MiCA authorisation explained](/learn/mica-casp-authorisation-explained/).

Lithuania chose a 12-month transitional period rather than MiCA's maximum of 18 months.[^grandfathering] MiCA allows member states to shorten the period "where they consider that their national regulatory framework applicable before 30 December 2024 is less strict than this Regulation."[^art143]

## Pros and cons

**In Lithuania's favour:**
- Recognisable international firms have chosen it as their EU base.

**Things to weigh:**
- Lithuania shortened the transition, a power MiCA gives where a state considers its old national regime less strict than MiCA.[^grandfathering][^art143]
- Only a small group of authorised firms so far, so there is little track record to compare against.
