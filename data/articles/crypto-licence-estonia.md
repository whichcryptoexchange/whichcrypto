For several years Estonia issued crypto licences through its Financial Intelligence Unit, an anti-money-laundering body rather than a financial supervisor. That regime is now closed. This matters for anyone looking at buying an Estonian company "with a crypto licence": the old licences no longer authorise anything.

## What happened to the old licences

The FIU announced that from 30 December 2024 it would stop issuing virtual currency service licences, and that Finantsinspektsioon would issue crypto-market licences instead. It said the change "tuleneb ELi küptovara määrusest (MiCA), mis allutab sektori finantsjärelevalve kontrollile" (follows from MiCA, which brings the sector under financial supervision).[^fiu-news]

At that point, 43 FIU licences remained valid.[^fiu-news] The transition then ran "kuni 1.07.2026, mil kaotasid kehtivuse kõik Rahapesu Andmebüroo väljastatud tegevusload" (until 1 July 2026, when all licences issued by the FIU lost their validity).[^fiu-vasp] The FIU also publishes a list of licences it has revoked, starting from 1 June 2021.[^fiu-revoked]

So an Estonian company holding only an FIU licence cannot provide crypto services today. A firm needs MiCA authorisation from Finantsinspektsioon, or a MiCA authorisation from another EU state passported into Estonia.

## Applying to Finantsinspektsioon

Since 18 March 2026, applications go through Finantsinspektsioon's application portal, and "the application must be submitted in Estonian."[^fi-apply] The processing fee for a crypto-asset service provider is €3,000.[^fi-apply]

Finantsinspektsioon checks completeness "within 25 working days", then assesses the application within 40 working days, a period that "may be paused for up to 20 working days."[^fi-apply] These are MiCA's own deadlines (see [MiCA authorisation explained](/learn/mica-casp-authorisation-explained/)). The application uses the EU standard form and document list.[^fi-apply]

In March 2026 Finantsinspektsioon also said applicants would "have to include with their application a plan for winding up their operations."[^fi-news]

Notifying cross-border services costs nothing, and you can start in other member states 15 calendar days after notifying.[^fi-apply]

## Who has done it

In March 2026 Finantsinspektsioon said it was "currently processing 10 applications for licences to provide crypto asset services."[^fi-news] Our register, built from ESMA's MiCA register, currently shows **{{home_count:EE}}** brands with an Estonian home-state entry: {{home_brands:EE:10}}.

Set that against the 43 FIU licences still valid at the end of 2024.[^fiu-news] On these figures, most firms that held an Estonian crypto licence have not, so far, become MiCA-authorised in Estonia.

## Pros and cons

**In Estonia's favour:**
- A fixed, published €3,000 fee and a digital application portal.[^fi-apply]
- Estonia kept MiCA's full 18-month transition, so firms had the longest available run-in.[^grandfathering]

**Things to weigh:**
- The application must be in Estonian.[^fi-apply]
- Few firms hold Estonian MiCA authorisation yet, so there is a thin track record to learn from.
- Only a Finantsinspektsioon (or passported MiCA) authorisation counts now. An FIU licence on its own authorises nothing.[^fiu-vasp]
