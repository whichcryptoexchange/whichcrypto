Germany is the most common home state on the EU's MiCA register. Many of those entries are German banks and brokers that notified BaFin rather than applying from scratch. This guide covers how authorisation works at BaFin, using BaFin's own published guidance and the German implementing law.

## The legal set-up

Germany implemented MiCA through the Kryptomärkteaufsichtsgesetz (KMAG) of 27 December 2024, which states that it "dient der Durchführung der Verordnung (EU) 2023/1114" (serves to implement MiCA).[^kmag] BaFin is the competent authority, and the Deutsche Bundesbank carries out ongoing supervision, as a rule through its regional offices.[^kmag]

The rules applicants must meet are MiCA's own: an EU registered office, effective management in the EU, a director resident in the EU, the application contents in Article 62, and minimum capital from €50,000 to €150,000. See our [MiCA explainer](/learn/mica-casp-authorisation-explained/). German law backs the substance rules with penalties: offering crypto services without the seat, management and resident director MiCA requires can draw a fine of up to €700,000.[^kmag]

## How the application works

BaFin asks applicants to arrange an initial meeting before filing: "Bitte vereinbaren Sie vor der formalen Antragseinreichung ein Erstgespräch" (please arrange an initial discussion before formally submitting your application).[^bafin]

You submit by post to BaFin in Bonn or electronically, with copies to BaFin's crypto team and the Bundesbank's competence centre.[^bafin] BaFin points to MiCA and its technical standards for the documents required, tells applicants to use the standard EU application form, and asks them to show they meet the EU's Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA).[^bafin]

BaFin first checks whether the file is complete. If not, it sets a four-week deadline and says it "kann und wird den Antrag ablehnen" (can and will reject the application) if the file is still incomplete afterwards.[^bafin] Once granted, the authorisation is published in the Federal Gazette, BaFin's company database and ESMA's interim MiCA register.[^bafin]

## Costs

There is no fixed fee. BaFin says "die Höhe der Gebühr richtet sich nach dem angefallenen Zeitaufwand" (the fee depends on the time spent), and that the fee applies "auch dann, wenn ein Antrag zurückgezogen oder abgewiesen wird" (even if an application is withdrawn or rejected).[^bafin] The hourly rates come from the federal fee regulations.[^findagebv] A poorly prepared file therefore costs more as well as taking longer.

## Firms already licensed in Germany

Firms with an existing German licence under the Banking Act (KWG) could use a simplified procedure. The law is explicit that it is no shortcut on substance: "Die Zulassung ist zu verweigern, wenn die Voraussetzungen des Titels V der Verordnung (EU) 2023/1114 nicht erfüllt sind" (authorisation must be refused if MiCA's Title V requirements are not met).[^kmag] Grandfathered permissions ended no later than 31 December 2025.[^kmag]

That is a 12-month transition, against MiCA's maximum of 18 months.[^grandfathering] MiCA allows member states to shorten the period "where they consider that their national regulatory framework applicable before 30 December 2024 is less strict than this Regulation."[^art143]

Many of Germany's entries are established banks and brokers adding crypto services, which is part of why Germany tops the table.

## Pros and cons

**In Germany's favour:**
- Ongoing supervision is shared with the Bundesbank's regional offices.[^kmag]
- Clear published guidance on the process, including the pre-filing meeting.[^bafin]
- The register shows established financial institutions choosing this route, which is useful context for banking and partner relationships.

**Things to weigh:**
- Fees are open-ended and payable even if you are refused.[^bafin]
- BaFin's MiCAR guidance note (Merkblatt) is published in German.[^merkblatt]
- BaFin says it will reject files that remain incomplete after the four-week deadline.[^bafin]
